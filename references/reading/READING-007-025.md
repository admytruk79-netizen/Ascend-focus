## Scan-checked passage I — method, PDF19 / printed7

Russian:
> Кромѣ того я просилъ бы васъ не принимать ничего на вѣру, разъ вы не можете доказать этого для удовлетворенія себя своими личными опытами послѣ того, какъ вами уже собраны достаточныя свѣдѣнія по этому предмету.

English translation of this passage: “Moreover, I would ask you not to accept
anything on faith if you cannot establish it to your own satisfaction through your
own experiments, after you have already gathered sufficient information on the
subject.” In context this is personal verification, not a specification of modern
controlled scientific experiments. A modern blinded/consensual protocol would be
an additional design, not a translation of this sentence.

## Scan-checked passage II — agency language, PDF23 / printed11

Russian:
> Теперь же ваше «не могу» я измѣняю въ положительное «могу» и еще въ болѣе рѣшительное «я хочу».

English: “Now I change your ‘cannot’ into the positive ‘can,’ and into the still more
decisive ‘I will.’” In this context «я хочу» emphasizes will/intention; literally it
can also mean “I want.” Retain that translation ambiguity rather than pretending
that the two English formulations are identical. The sentence is the author's
announced instructional intent; it is not a reader's timed repetition prescription.

## Scan-checked passage III — suggested thought, PDF25 / printed13

Russian:
> Но подумайте: «я не боюсь», и каждая смѣлая мысль въ окружающей васъ средѣ, какъ сила, устремится на васъ и будетъ вамъ помогать. Испробуйте это, въ особенности же послѣднее. Устраните трусливыя мысли.

English: “But think, ‘I am not afraid,’ and every courageous thought in your
surroundings, as a force, will rush toward you and help you. Try this, especially
the latter. Remove fearful thoughts.” The first sentence contains the author's
unverified attraction-of-thought claim. The invitation to try the phrase is genuine
source advice; its explanation is not scientifically established. No duration,
count, interval or result measure is supplied.

The later “reflect for a minute” wording on the same page is ordinary rhetorical
language in an argument, not a complete one-minute training prescription. It must
not silently become an exercise default. The author's exhortation to eliminate
fear/hatred does not justify thought suppression, blaming anxiety or claiming a
medical intervention. Any gentle present-day agency reflection would be separately
labelled as a modern adaptation and reviewed before publication.
