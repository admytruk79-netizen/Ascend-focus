## Heading and relationship to Lesson VII

PDF46 / printed34: «Шестая лекція. Сила глазъ.»
English: “Sixth lesson. The power of the eyes.” The author describes gaze as a means
of maintaining attention, conveying will and allegedly transmitting thought
vibrations directly to another brain. He promises gaze-development exercises in
the next lesson and explicitly assumes, for the current discussion, that the reader
already possesses the so-called magnetic gaze. This is a narrative assumption,
not a demonstrated capability, verified prerequisite or actual app assessment.

Lesson VI concerns **application in conversation**. Lesson VII's paper-point,
mirror and other numbered exercises must not be imported into this chapter's
procedure or timing. Its fixed-gaze language is not scientific proof of hypnosis,
thought transmission or improvement in eyesight.

## Direct gaze during requests — PDF47 / printed35

The original distinguishes a firm/persistent gaze from merely staring and allows
changes of gaze direction during ordinary conversation. It nevertheless demands
decisive direct eye contact for proposals and requests, intended to express that
the other should comply. Neither the distinction nor this context supplies a
comfortable eye-contact duration or a no-blinking instruction in this lesson.

Scan-checked sentence:
> Всѣми средствами помѣшайте ему въ эту минуту отвернуться отъ васъ.

English: “By every means prevent him from turning away from you at that moment.”
Here «въ эту минуту» is “at that moment,” not a one-minute practice timer. The
coercive instruction is preserved for documentary fidelity and excluded from
modern guided publication. Optional eye contact and the freedom to look away
would be modern safeguards, not the wording of this original passage.

## Recapturing gaze and using an object — PDF48 / printed36

The source describes two alternative tactics: watching while looking aside and
returning a sharp gaze when the person glances back; or showing a sample, picture
or business-related object and then catching the resulting glance toward the
practitioner. Neither is a numbered exercise or a screen-target motion pattern.

Scan-checked clause for the object alternative:
> то очень хорошо будетъ имѣть что-нибудь подъ рукою, чтобы показать ему: образчикъ, рисунокъ или какой-нибудь предметъ, относящійся къ вашему дѣлу.

English: “Then it would be very useful to have something at hand to show him:
a sample, a picture, or some object related to your business.” The surrounding
gaze-capture purpose must be retained. This is not interchangeable with Lesson
XIV's ordinary-object/pencil observation merely because both mention objects.
No covert gaze-capture task or manipulation of an unwilling person is published.

## Preventing the conversation from ending — PDF48–49 / printed36–37

The condition begins near the end of PDF48; its instruction continues on PDF49.
The source says that someone feeling the influence may wish to end the conversation
for self-protection, and that the practitioner should not allow it.

Scan-checked sentence, PDF49:
> Не оставляйте его до тѣхъ поръ, пока не добьетесь того, за чѣмъ пришли.

English: “Do not leave him until you obtain what you came for.” This is historical
coercive advice, not a consent-based completion rule. Ending participation and
conversation must remain voluntary in any modern adaptation. No such guided task
is activated here.

## Self-protection sequence — PDF49–50 / printed37–38

This is contiguous advice in prose, not a source-numbered exercise. Faithful
normalized sequence:

1. Keep a steady mental attitude, with the author's belief that one is not subject
   to the supposed influence.
2. Do not let the other continuously hold one's gaze; look aside naturally while
   considering a proposal, to think and retain composure.
3. If an unexpected proposal catches one's gaze, do not answer until looking aside
   **“for a minute”** and restoring equilibrium.
4. If the answer is no, look directly into the other's eyes and say no firmly,
   clearly and politely.
5. When in doubt, say no; keep the metaphorical active partner alert and prohibit
   the speaker from addressing the passive one.

Scan-checked sentence, PDF49:
> Но если же все-таки ему удастся сдѣлать вамъ внезапное внушеніе или предложеніе, поймавъ вашъ взглядъ, то не отвѣчайте ему, пока не отведете своихъ глазъ на минуту въ сторону и не возстановите своего духовнаго равновѣсія.

English: “But if he nevertheless manages to make a sudden suggestion or proposal,
having caught your gaze, do not answer him until you have turned your eyes aside
for a minute and restored your mental equilibrium.” «На минуту» is preserved as
the source's timing phrase. It may function conversationally as a brief pause;
there is no recurring stopwatch schedule, repetition count or advancement rule.
The phrase is not removed from history or automatically assigned a60-second app
timer. It applies to the response pause, not the whole conversation or gaze session.

Scan-checked sentence, PDF50:
> Находясь въ сомнѣніи, слѣдуетъ всегда говорить «нѣтъ».

English: “When in doubt, one should always say ‘no.’” The preceding continuation
specifies a firm, clear but polite refusal. The author's instruction to respect
one's own refusal sits alongside instructions not to permit the other to stop.
That asymmetry remains visible rather than being rewritten as a consistent modern
consent system.

Any contemporary agency/pause adaptation would make eye contact optional and
frame the pause as time to consider a decision, not evidence that somebody is
transmitting thoughts or controlling one's mind. This is a modern difference;
no claim of protective efficacy against psychic influence follows from the source.

## Listener-receptivity advice — PDF50 / printed38

The author calls the speaker positive and the attentive listener increasingly
passive, saying the positive is stronger than the negative. He therefore advises
avoiding reception of positive suggestions when oneself is in a passive position.
Here positive/negative refer to his dominant/receptive influence model, not to
pleasant/unpleasant emotions or a scientifically validated assessment of a user.
The extra listener-caution record preserves this advice without treating attentive
listening as proof that another person can control the listener's mind.

## Firm suggestions and the imagined quality — PDF50 / printed38

Scan-checked sentence:
> Вы должны научиться давать внушенія въ настойчивой, твердой, положительной формѣ;

English: “You must learn to give suggestions in a persistent, firm, positive form.”
The following text asks for a voice expecting fulfillment and confidence in
compliance. This is a general influence prescription, not a measured training task.

Scan-checked phrase:
> увѣренная настойчивость

English: “Confident persistence.” The author asks the reader to form a mental image
of the meaning expressed by these two words to understand how to give suggestions.
No object, image stages, practice repetitions or timer is specified. Do not invent
those details, equate the phrase with Lesson XII's six-stage sequence, or imply
that confidence guarantees another person's compliance.
