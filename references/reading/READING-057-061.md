## Specialized terminology, PDF57–58 / printed45–46

The main text defines conscious will-effort as producing thought vibrations and
directing them toward an object, then names it «хотѣніе». It explicitly distinguishes
that usage from «желаніе». Here the first is rendered **will-directed intent**;
ordinary “wanting” would erase the source's distinction. This label describes the
historical author's concept, not a scientifically established transmission process.

The footnote on58 gives English/Latin forms including the legible “volation,”
“volic” and “volition,” and relates them to «хочу». One additional Latin-letter
form in the note is not transcribed here pending close proofreading. The note is
unsigned; its individual author or whether each line originated with Atkinson is
not inferred. Unlike the signed editorial note on29–30, this note does not provide
an explicit authorship declaration. No proprietary cognitive algorithm is inferred
from these terms.

## Actual unnumbered contemplation, PDF59 / printed47

Scan-checked Russian instruction:
> Отложите въ сторону эту книгу на нѣсколько минутъ и, ослабивъ мускулы вашего тѣла съ приведеніемъ въ пассивное состояніе вашего ума, спокойно и хладнокровно думайте о словахъ «я есмь», и попробуйте представить себѣ ваше истинное «я», стоящее надъ разумомъ и тѣломъ.

English: “Put this book aside for a few minutes and, relaxing the muscles of your
body while bringing your mind into a passive state, think calmly and composedly
about the words ‘I am,’ and try to imagine your true self standing above mind
and body.” «Я есмь» is the older Russian form of “I am.”

Faithful normalized source sequence:
1. Set the book aside for a few minutes.
2. Relax bodily muscles and bring the mind into the source's described passive state.
3. Calmly consider “I am,” and try to imagine the true self above mind and body.
4. Continue the practice. The author predicts recognition of the true self and says
   it remains eternal/indestructible even when body and mind disappear.

The fourth item's prediction is historical attribution, not an app achievement
criterion. The text says **a few minutes**; it gives no exact initial number,
repetition count, daily schedule or advancement threshold. “Continue the practice”
is qualitative continuation, not a fixed timed progression. This is an explicitly
instructed, unnumbered practice; it is not fabricated from the chapter's theory.

It differs from Lesson X's protective personal-agency passage and Lesson XII's
six-stage quality-imagery sequence. Those must not share the same source attribution
merely because each uses an “I am” expression.

## Textual tension on PDF60 / printed48

The scan reads:
> Воля достаточно сильна и нуждается въ «развитіи».

English, preserving the printed wording: “Will is sufficiently strong and needs
‘development.’” The preceding account treats development as the mind recognizing
will, and the following sentence says this conflicts with a common view. A tempting
reading would insert «не» (“does not need”), but **that word is not visible in the
checked printed sentence**. It is not supplied from presumed meaning or another
edition. Whether this is a translation/printing error requires independent edition
comparison. This line is indexed as an unresolved textual issue, not a new exercise
or a premise the guided adaptation depends on.

The poem and its footnote are literary illustration, not timed stages or exercises;
its attribution is the credit printed in this scan, not independently verified
literary provenance.
