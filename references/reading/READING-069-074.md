## Six numbered experiments

1. 69–70: Walk behind a person about6–10 feet away (greater distance allowed by
   author), gaze at the back of the head/base of brain and will a turn toward you.
   Claimed sex differences on70 are not scientifically established here.
2. 70: In a church/theatre/concert or similar gathering, fix the same gaze point
   on someone ahead and will them to turn. Author predicts restlessness and claims
   better results with acquaintances. Seating is not explicitly prescribed.
3. 70–71: Across a street, slightly offset from opposite, conceal that you are
   watching while keeping the person in the visual field; mentally demand a look.
   Corrects earlier shorthand “without gaze”; observation remains in the protocol.
4. 71: When a speaker searches for a word, gaze and mentally suggest a suitable
   word. Public-speaker stories and the examination anecdote continuing72 are
   anecdotes, not additional numbered experiments or controlled evidence.
5. 72–73: Attempt to direct pedestrian passing right/left; includes walking behind
   someone and a variant approaching someone without changing one's own path.
   First variant's pronoun antecedent remains ambiguous; no silent reconstruction.
   These are historical manipulation instructions, not guided street activities.
6. 73: From a window will a passerby to look; lower floor favored. Reverse variant
   has the practitioner pass someone at a window. “Seven out of ten” is the
   author's asserted outcome, not required repetitions, baseline or verified rate.
