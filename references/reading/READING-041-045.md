«Пятая лекція. Нѣсколько житейской мудрости.»
English: “Fifth lesson. Some practical wisdom.” «Житейской» denotes everyday/worldly

partner analogy from Lesson IV, treating social conduct as a means of influence.

## Listening — PDF41–42 / printed29–30

Scan-checked sentence, PDF41:
> Говорите съ нимъ до тѣхъ поръ, пока не натолкнете его на его излюбленную тему, и тогда—молчите.

English: “Speak with him until you bring him to his favorite subject, and then—be
silent.” The subsequent sentence recommends cultivating the art of listening as a
valuable life talent.

Scan-checked closing instruction, PDF42:
> Итакъ всѣми средствами развивайте въ себѣ искусство умѣло слушать.

English: “Thus, by every means, develop the art of listening skillfully.” The
surrounding text recommends respectful attention but withholding susceptibility
to the speaker's ideas, and anticipating access to the metaphorical passive partner
when the active one relaxes vigilance. Those strategic elements belong to the
historical method; omitting them while labelling modern empathic listening as the
original would misstate the source.

Source procedure, normalized: identify conversational interests; raise a preferred
topic without speaking excessively; stop speaking and listen attentively; maintain
one's own firmness while the other talks. Original purpose: interpersonal influence
within the active/passive model. No duration, number of conversations, repetition
schedule, reflection form or advancement criterion is prescribed.

The Carlyle anecdote's **three hours** is the reported length of that conversation,
not an exercise duration or recommended endurance. A consent-based listening
exercise focused on understanding, mutual turns and accurate summarization would
be a modern addition, not a silent translation of this passage.

## Appearance and manner — PDF42–44 / printed30–32

PDF42, scan-checked sentence:
> Платье должно быть просто, чисто и изящно.

English: “Clothing should be simple, clean and tasteful.” The passage also discusses
hat, polished shoes, clean linen and avoiding strong perfume. Its class/gender norms
are historical; the app does not make appearance, spending, perfume or clothing
compliance a prerequisite for attention practice.

PDF43, scan-checked clause:
> Сдержанность въ нѣкоторой степени всегда желательна, а также и умѣніе управлять своимъ настроеніемъ.

English: “Restraint to some degree is always desirable, as is the ability to manage
one's mood.” The author pairs this with fearlessness and a reference to the lesson
on developing character. No specific emotional-regulation procedure is supplied
here. These prescriptions do not justify blaming anxious/irritable readers or
suppressing necessary movement/emotion in guided practice.

The same page asks for self-respect together with regard for others' feelings and
tastes. PDF44 continues with open manners and seriousness of speech; its claims
about strengthening thought waves are not scientifically established outcomes.

## Reciprocal treatment as thought and action — PDF44 / printed32

Scan-checked phrase:
> «Я буду поступать въ отношеніи васъ такъ, какъ хотѣлъ бы, чтобы вы поступали со мной»

English: “I will act toward you as I would wish you to act toward me.” The sentence
asks the reader to keep this thought in mind and turn it into actual behavior.
This is an authentic thought/action prescription, not a numbered timed exercise.
It supplies no daily quota, fixed reflection prompt or timer. Any app reflection
or contemporary consent policy would remain an explicitly modern layer.

## Handshake and eye contact — PDF44 / printed32

The source recommends developing a firm handshake and looking directly into the
other person's eyes, describing these together as mutually complementary.

Scan-checked sentence:
> Затѣмъ всякому смотрите прямо въ глаза.

English: “Then look everyone directly in the eyes.” This is not an instruction to
run the black-dot exercise or proof of a magnetic gaze. No hold duration or eye-
contact repetition count is given here. Physical touch and direct eye contact are
not mandatory in any modern accessible adaptation; consent, alternatives and
comfort would be added safeguards, not original qualifications in this paragraph.
The source's judgments of weak handshakes and its gender/class comparison are not
adopted as app judgments of character or disability.

## Voice procedure — PDF44–45 / printed32–33

PDF44, scan-checked sentence:
> Если вашъ собесѣдникъ дѣйствительно будетъ говорить громко, то понизьте тонъ вашего голоса, и онъ вскорѣ заговоритъ также спокойно.

English: “If your conversational partner is indeed speaking loudly, lower the tone
of your voice, and he will soon speak calmly as well.” In context, «тонъ» concerns
voice level/quality in contrast with shouting; no numerical pitch or loudness is
specified. The instruction is paired with remaining calm and speaking restrainedly
but confidently. PDF45 continues its predicted effect and says to try it.

Source sequence: use a pleasant, expressive voice; avoid mumbling and sharp shouting;
adjust to the other person's voice without trying to outshout it; when the other
is loud, lower one's own voice and stay calm/confident. Claimed outcome: the other's
voice/excitement falls, they feel ashamed, and the practitioner gains an advantage.
The outcome is Atkinson's prediction, not guaranteed or independently established
by this reading. There is no specified time, repetition count, stopping rule,
measured scoring system or trial design.

A modern de-escalation adaptation would prioritize voluntary communication and
safety, not shame or winning, and would allow leaving. That would be a modern
change. No task to confront distressed people, strangers or an unsafe situation
is added to the runtime catalog.

## Closing study advice — PDF45 / printed33

The author says not to despair if these qualities are absent and refers readers to
character-development material. He promises the next lesson on use of gaze and
resisting other people's gaze. These are study pointers and historical confidence
claims. They do not provide missing training steps or establish that every person
can or should use the same speech, gaze or physical greeting.
