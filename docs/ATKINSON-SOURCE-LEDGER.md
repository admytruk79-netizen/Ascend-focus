## Lesson VII — Magnetic gaze (PDF pp. 51–56, printed pp. 39–44)
- VII-1 Black point (PDF 52–53): Draw a black circle on white paper at eye level, hold visual attention, move target right and left; historical instruction discourages blinking and increases durations. Modern app: allow blinking and brief optional sessions; never force staring or strain.
- VII-2 Mirror gaze (PDF 53–54): Look at one's own eyes in a mirror. Modern app: nonstraining self-observation.
- VII-3 Head motion/fixed mark (PDF 54): Keep target under observation while moving head slowly. Modern app: allow opt-out, stop dizziness.
- VII-4 Moving attention between targets (PDF 54): Shift gaze among points on a wall; source says to stop when fatigued. Modern app: adjustable slow motion and reduced-motion setting.
- VII-5 Partner gaze (PDF 54–55): Atkinson suggests partner gaze and experiments with animals. App: only consensual, comfortable human partner; no distressing animals.

## Lesson IX — Will and attempted influence (PDF pp. 69–74, printed pp. 57–62)
Six historical experiments are described. Their purported remote influence effects are unverified and should never be implemented as covert behavioral manipulation.
- IX-1 PDF 69–70: Imagine prompting a person walking ahead to turn.
- IX-2 PDF 70: Imagine drawing the attention of a seated person.
- IX-3 PDF 70–71: Attempt to attract another's attention without gaze.
- IX-4 PDF 71: Imagine transmitting a word to a person searching for it.
- IX-5 PDF 72–73: Imagine influencing movement direction.
- IX-6 PDF 73: From a window imagine a passerby looking toward you.
Modern adaptations: private visualization, optional consenting paired word/picture guessing, and pre-registered blind trials; not stranger targeting.

## Lesson X — Telepathic Will (PDF pp. 75–85, printed pp. 63–73)
- X-1 PDF 79: Find a quiet place; sit or lie comfortably; relax muscles and settle thoughts.
- X-2 PDF 79: Form a mental image of another person; historical text treats vividness as evidence of rapport, which is not established.
- X-3 PDF 79–80: Imagine the person's behavior as mental communication.
- X-4 PDF 80–81: Visualize a long hollow tube/corridor connecting imagined people as a concentration method.
- X-5 PDF 81: Affirm personal agency ('I am') as a historical mental-boundary technique.
Modern adaptation must not claim telepathic communication is scientifically established or encourage belief in unwanted external thought intrusion.

## Lesson XIV — Concentration (PDF pp. 120–125 and continuing; printed pp. 108–113+)
- XIV-A1 PDF 120–121: Relaxed seated stillness, historically 5 then 10 then 15 minutes. App allows comfortable movement.
- XIV-A2 PDF 121–122: Observe the fingers of an extended arm, starting with 1 minute; stop physical discomfort.
- XIV-A3 PDF 122 (printed p. 110): Fill a small glass to the brim, hold it with the fingers, extend the arm in front, and watch the liquid for steadiness. Start with one minute, gradually increase to five, alternate hands. Safer app uses a partially filled unbreakable container and optional arm support. Do not attribute this exercise to PDF p. 121, which describes A2.
- XIV-A4 PDF 122–123: Observe movement habits; do not stigmatize fidgeting/neurodivergence.
- XIV-B1 PDF 123–124 (printed pp. 111–112), newly checked against the scan: Rest the hands on a table, fists palm-up with thumb across fingers. Move the thumb away, unfold the fingers in order, then reverse from the little finger, and repeat with the other hand. Five repetitions per session, later ten; sustained attention on the movement. No numerical timer is stated. Guided range, order and repetitions are comfort-based modern adaptations.
- XIV-B2 PDF 124: Interlace fingers, leave thumbs free, rotate one thumb slowly around the other while attending to their tips. No timer or repetition count stated.
- XIV-B3 PDF 124: Extend index finger and move side to side while attending to it.
- XIV-C PDF 125–126 (printed pp. 113–114): Study a pencil or another ordinary, uninteresting object for five minutes; look, turn, investigate qualities, use, materials and manufacture. PDF p. 126 continues the instruction and explains choosing an ordinary rather than especially interesting object. Gentle refocusing is a modern adaptation.

## Primary-scan verification additions (10 October 2026)

- VII-1 PDF 52: Original one-minute gaze bouts; approximately five central repetitions, four rightward repetitions and five leftward repetitions, with eye rest. Right/left paper positions are three feet from the original position and the head remains facing forward. After three days use two minutes, after another three days use three, then add one minute every three days; the author advises a fifteen-minute limit. The no-blinking instruction is documented, not recommended. The app's two-minute default, natural blinking and screen tracking are explicitly modern choices.
- VII-2 PDF 53: Stand before a mirror, gaze at the reflection of one's eyes as in exercise I, and gradually increase time. This is a cross-reference, not a separately specified mirror timer. The continued recommendation to combine the practices is on PDF p. 54.
- X-1 through X-4 PDF 79–81: Quiet rest, relaxed muscles, person imagery, imagining the person carrying out the reader's wishes, and a tube about one foot in diameter. The text says the tube is optional. No numerical session duration is stated. Consent, fictional-person alternatives, kind conversation and a four-minute app timer are modern additions. Historical influence claims are not established facts.
- XIV-A1 PDF 121: Five, then ten, then fifteen minutes once the preceding duration is easy; muscles relaxed, not rigid; an explicit warning against overfatigue.
- XIV-A2 PDF 121–122: Right arm extended sideways at shoulder level, then left; palm down, attention on fingertips. One minute initially, then two, three, and eventually five; no daily progression schedule is stated.
